# How the cure tracker works

## People and roles

- **Everyone signed in** sees every person on the list and every note. Access decides who gets in; there is no per-person privacy inside the app.
- **Volunteer**: anyone Access lets in. Their first visit asks for a name. They can claim an unclaimed person, release their own claims, set a status, choose how they reached the person, and add notes on their own people.
- **Admin**: an email in the `ADMIN_EMAILS` Worker secret (`npx wrangler secret put ADMIN_EMAILS`, comma-separated; docs/setup.md). Admins can do all of the above on anyone's people, plus: upload the sheet, move one person or all of a volunteer's open people to someone else (or back to the pool), dismiss flags, read the audit log.
- **Precinct organizer**: a volunteer a site admin has given precincts to (some of them, or all), on the admin page under "Who organizes precincts". Grants are rows in the `organizers` table, so they change without a deploy. An organizer gets a trimmed admin page: the numbers, the flagged people, the volunteers and "find someone and move them", all limited to their precincts. They can move people of those precincts to any volunteer (or back to the pool), move all of a volunteer's open people in those precincts, and dismiss flags there. People whose row has no precinct can be moved by anyone who organizes anything. An organizer cannot upload, look up addresses, place people by hand, change settings, read the audit log, start over, or change who organizes. A site admin organizes every precinct. Every grant and every move is in the audit log. A grant can only be for a precinct on the district's list (`src/precincts.ts`, 56 precincts with their cities, from Avi), compared ignoring case and extra spaces and kept as the list spells it. An upload's preview warns about any precinct on the sheet that is not on the list, since those people can be moved only by a site admin or an organizer of all precincts.
- A service token (a machine caller) is refused: the app needs an email.

## Statuses

| Status | Set by | Meaning |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Needs to be contacted | upload (new person), volunteer | nobody has reached them |
| Left a message | volunteer | tried, no conversation: a voicemail, a text, or instructions left at the door; with no method chosen, it counts as a phone call |
| Contacted | volunteer | the volunteer talked to them, or gave the cure steps to someone at the door; the ballot should be cured. A promise to cure is not a cure: only the sheet no longer listing them is |
| Cured | upload only | the state's sheet no longer lists them |

How they reached the person is a separate choice: phone call, text, phone call and text, voicemail and text, email, in person (spoke with the voter, witnessed the cure, spoke with someone else at the address, or left instructions at the door), or other. It does not change the status: "witnessed the cure" is a claim by the volunteer, so the person stays Contacted until the state's sheet stops listing them, and only then is Cured.

A volunteer can never set Cured: only the state's data can verify it. Claims do not expire and there is no limit.

## The sheet

The admin exports the LD8 tab of the state's workbook as CSV. Columns are matched by name, in any order, ignoring case and anything that is not a letter or digit (`Precinct Name` and `PRECINCT_NAME` are the same), and extra columns are ignored. `FIRST`, `LAST` and `ADDRESS` are required (also `FIRST NAME`, `LAST NAME`, `RESIDENCE ADDRESS`); `MID`, `STATUSDATE`, `PARTY`, `LEGDIST`, `REASONCODE`, `PHONE` and `PRECINCT_NAME` are used when present, and a warning says what is blank when one is missing. When the state renames a column, the upload page lists the file's columns with a select for each field, with an example value from the first row, so the admin can say which is which. What an upload used is saved (the `column_map` setting) and is the starting point for the next one while the file still has that header; the upload's audit row records it. The delimiter is a comma, or a tab for a sheet pasted as text.

- Rows with a `LEGDIST` other than 8 are skipped (a blank `LEGDIST` is taken as 8).
- The sheet has no voter id, so **a person is their normalized first name, last name and address** (`normalizeKey` in `src/sheet.ts`). Names keep only letters and digits, so `O'Brien`, `O Brien` and `OBRIEN` are one name; accents and the middle name do not count. The address is reduced to the street line, so the state, ZIP (with or without +4), a corrected ZIP or city, and the word before a unit number (`APT 2129`, `UNIT 2129`, `# 2129`, `2129`) do not make a new person, and the words the state spells differently between dumps are made the same (`STREET`/`ST`, `NORTH`/`N`, `P.O. BOX`/`PO BOX`, after USPS Publication 28). Different unit numbers, and a unit left out entirely, are different keys; the second is what **possible matches** are for. The stored and displayed address is still the sheet's own. A person with the same first and last name at the same street address but a different suffix (Jr, Sr) is not merged by the key. If the rules above change, `KEY_VERSION` goes up and the upload page warns that the people already here were keyed the old way (use **Start over** first).
- The city is read from the address (`3302 N 47TH PL PHOENIX, AZ 85018`) by matching the end against a list of Maricopa-area cities; an unknown city is left blank and the preview says so. Add the city to `CITIES` in `worker/import.ts`.
- The status date (`10/21/2026`, `2026-10-21`, with or without a time) gives "days uncured": the days from it to today in Arizona.
- A row repeated within a file is merged: the later status date wins, blanks are filled from the other row.

### Where people live

An admin presses **Look up addresses** (admin page, "Where people live"). The Worker sends each open person's street address (an id, street, city, state and ZIP; never a name, phone number or note) to the US Census Bureau's batch geocoder, two hundred at a time, and stores the coordinates it answers with in `people` (`lat`, `lon`, `geocode`, `geocoded_at`). The page first shows how many are waiting and the first lines of the next request. A failed request stores nothing and the run goes on where it stopped; each request has a `geocode` audit row. Cured people are never sent. Locally (the dev server, the tests) a stand-in answers, so nothing reaches the network.

**What is sent.** The street is sent without its unit: `1500 W RIO SALADO PKWY 109`, `... APT 109` and `... # 109` all go as `1500 W RIO SALADO PKWY`. The Census ignores a unit it understands and can fail on one written as a bare number after the street type (a test against the real geocoder: `1600 W La Jolla Dr 1064` missed, `1600 W La Jolla Dr` matched). A number that is part of the name (`HWY 87`) or a street with no recognizable type is left alone.

**Exact and rough places.** A match the Census calls *Exact* is placed exactly. One it calls *Non_Exact* (the street found, the number or the side of the street guessed) is stored as a place but marked `approximate = 1`, like the middle of a ZIP, so the map draws it as a guess.

**People the geocoder cannot place** (`geocode` is `no_match`, or `tie` when it found several places) are not sent again by a normal run. When a run ends, each of them is put at the middle of their ZIP, the average of the people the geocoder placed exactly there, if at least five were, and marked `approximate = 1`; otherwise they have no place. The admin page lists them (50 at a time, with the reason and what they have for now), with a **Copy** button for each address and a download of the whole list as a CSV, for looking them up by hand. **Look up the unplaced again** sends only them, each twice: as before, and the street alone with no city or ZIP, which finds a street the sheet puts in a different town from the Census's (a boundary: `1500 W Rio Salado Pkwy` is Tempe 85281 to the Census and matched exactly alone, but missed as `Mesa 85201`). The second answer is believed only if it is within 15 miles of the middle of the person's ZIP (and only if that ZIP has a middle): the Census can find the same street and number in another town, though it answers a tie where several match. One now found is placed (exactly, or roughly if non-exact); one still missed keeps its guess, or the place an admin gave it. An upload that changes a person's address clears their coordinates and the approximate flag, so they are looked up again.

**Guessed places can be corrected too.** The hand-place list (admin page, "Places to check by hand") holds not only the people the geocoder could not place but also those it placed only roughly (`geocode` is `match` and `approximate = 1`: a Non_Exact match, shown orange on the map, which can land far from the real address), listed as "Close match, not exact". A search box finds a person by name or address (`GET /api/admin/geocode/unplaced?q=`). "Look up the unplaced again" still sends only the people with no answer. Someone placed by hand stays on the list so the place can be corrected. A place set by hand is still drawn as a guess on the map (the orange pin, and "about" before the miles in a distance search), by Avi's choice, though it counts as placed exactly in the numbers on the admin page.

**Placing someone by hand.** In the list, each person has a box for coordinates (write latitude, then longitude, as a map gives them: `33.4295, -111.9628`) with **Set** and, once set, **Clear** (`POST /api/admin/people/:id/place`). The place is stored to about a metre and counts as placed exactly, recorded in `people.placed_by` with the admin's email and in the audit log (which says which person and who, not where). The geocoder's verdict stays as it was, so the person stays in the list, to be corrected or taken away. A later lookup that misses leaves the hand-set place alone; one that finds the person replaces it and clears "by hand"; an upload that changes the address clears it with the rest. Only a place set by hand can be cleared this way. A cured person cannot be placed.

**For the map:** approximate places are drawn differently from exact ones, with a legend saying so (Avi's requirement; see **The map**, below).

### Near me

The filters on Find people (search, city, precinct, party, sort) sit in one row. Choosing a city narrows the precincts offered to that city's (a precinct that crosses a city line is under both), and choosing a precinct narrows the cities; the upload stores which precincts each city has, with the other filter values. The precinct matches float under their field, so opening them moves nothing. The controls about the volunteer's own place sit in a labelled **Near me** box under the filters.

On Find people a volunteer can save where they are, from their device (the browser asks first) or as the middle of a ZIP (the average of the people placed exactly there, if at least five). It is rounded to 0.01 degrees, about 0.7 mile, before it is stored, shown only to that volunteer (not in the volunteer list, not to admins), never written to the audit log (which says only that a place was saved or forgotten), and cleared with **Forget where I am**. Once a place is saved the page says where it is in words ("near Phoenix 85018": the town and ZIP of the nearest person waiting, if anyone is within 25 miles) and the ways to save one (**Use my location**, the ZIP box) go away; **Change** brings them back and **Cancel** puts them away again. With one saved, **Show people within 1, 3, 5, 10 or 25 miles** filters the pool, **Nearest first** sorts by distance, and each row shows how far ("0.4 mi", or "about 2.0 mi" for someone who only has the middle of their ZIP). A radius is required to sort by distance, because a distance sort over everyone would read the whole pool; the search reads a band of the map from an index on the pool's latitude and longitude (`people_pool_place`). People with no place at all never appear in a distance search; they are still in the plain list. Distances are worked out in SQL on a flat map with plain arithmetic (D1 refuses SQLite's math functions; the difference from great-circle distance is under a hundredth of a percent), and checked in the tests against great-circle distances. The map's radius is the same distance, so the pins within 3 miles are the people the list finds within 3 miles.

### What an upload does

### What an upload does

The admin picks the file and presses **Check the file**. The browser reads the CSV (`src/sheet.ts`) and sends the people to the Worker a hundred at a time (`src/upload.ts`), because a free Worker has about 10 ms of CPU a request and could not read a large file; the page shows the progress. They wait in a scratch table (`upload_rows`); no person is changed. The page then shows the counts, warnings and possible matches, which the database works out by comparing the staged people with the people (SQL in `worker/uploads.ts`). **Upload this file** applies it, all or nothing, in one batch, without sending the file again. Only one upload is in flight: choosing a file again replaces the staged one, and the upload refuses to apply unless every person arrived. Because the people are read in key order, ids follow the key, not the sheet's order.

1. A person not seen before is added as **Needs to be contacted**, unclaimed.
2. A known person on the sheet has their details refreshed (phone, address, precinct, status date, ...). Their status and volunteer stay, with two exceptions that **flag** them:
   - **Unsuccessful cure** (flag `still_uncured`): a volunteer marked them Contacted, and the sheet still lists them after the **grace period**. They go back to Needs to be contacted, keep their volunteer, and show on the admin panel. The grace is how many uploads may still list a contacted person before this counts: an admin sets it under **Unsuccessful cures** on the admin page, from 0 (the first upload that lists them) to 30, and it starts at 1, because the state's data runs a little behind the phone calls. Each upload that lists a contacted person is one cycle; changing someone's status starts the count over.
   - **Back after being cured**: a Cured person is on the sheet again. They are reopened as Needs to be contacted, keep their old volunteer, and are flagged.
3. A person who is not on the sheet and not yet Cured becomes **Cured**. They keep their volunteer, so the credit stays.
4. A volunteer answering the flag (changing the status) or an admin dismissing it clears it.

Safeguards:
- A file that would cure more than half of at least ten open people is probably a partial export. The upload is refused unless the admin ticks "Upload it anyway".
- A new person who looks like someone who just disappeared is listed as a **possible match**: the same last name and address with another first name, the same first name and address with another last name, or the same name at another address. The match is by the normalized key, not by similarity: a typo in two of the three parts, a nickname, or `ST` against `STREET` is not found. Each listed pair has a **same person** box. Ticking it (the Worker keeps the marks with the staged sheet and works the counts out again) makes the upload re-key the old record to the sheet's key and update it, instead of curing it and adding the new one as a stranger: the record keeps its id, notes, volunteer, status and history, and an `import.merged` audit row says who they were. Unticked, nothing is merged, as before.

## Start over

While trying the tool with test data, an admin can use **Start over** (bottom of the admin page) to delete every voter, every note and the upload history. A dialog asks first. Volunteers, the grace period setting and the audit log stay, and the log gets a row saying how much was deleted. It exists only where the deployment turns it on: `ALLOW_RESET` in `wrangler.jsonc` is `"true"`; set it to `"false"` and deploy before the real sheet goes in, and the route refuses and the section disappears. The API also requires the body `{ "confirm": "delete all voters" }`, so a stray request cannot do it.

## The map

**Assigning people.** An organizer (a site admin, or a volunteer with precincts) sees an **Assign to...** box with an **Assign** button on Find people (a column in the table, a line in each phone card) and in the Map's pin panel, for each person in their precincts who is not cured (`POST /api/admin/people/:id/assign`, the same route as the admin page's move; the server checks the precinct). The person leaves the pool and is on that volunteer's list. The volunteer is not emailed at that moment: the assignment marks the person as waiting to be told (`people.notify_pending`), and the admin page's **Tell volunteers about new people** lists each volunteer with the number of new people (in the organizer's precincts). **Send email** (or **Send all**) sends one message from `admin@azld8cures.com` per volunteer, however many people: who assigned them voters and a link to the site, with no voter name, address or phone in it (an HTML message in the LD8 colors with a "View my list" button, and a plain-text copy; replies go to the sender). The marks clear for exactly the people the email covered; a failed send loses nothing and can be tried again. Giving a person to yourself, the pool, a volunteer who has them already, claiming or releasing clears or keeps the mark as appropriate, and a cured person is never counted. Every volunteer has a verified email already: a volunteer row exists only once someone has signed in through Access, which proved the address (a one-time PIN sent to it, or Google). Each send is audited (`notify.send`).

**The admin page is four tabs** (Frostline tabs, one page at `/admin/`; the tab is in the address, so `/admin/#data` opens People data): **Overview** (the numbers, who to tell about new people, flagged people, volunteers, find someone and move them), **People data** (upload, where people live, phone numbers, the export for the old spreadsheet), **Access** (who organizes which precincts, inviting people) and **Settings and log** (the grace period, start over, the audit log). A precinct organizer sees only Overview and People data, and in People data only the phone numbers and the export. Every tab's content loads when the page opens.

**Feedback.** Every page's account menu has **Send feedback**, a page (`/feedback/`) with one box. It posts to `POST /api/feedback`, and the Worker emails the message to `admin@azld8cures.com` (`FEEDBACK_TO` in `worker/mail.ts`) from the app's sender address, with the sender's name and email and the page they came from, and replies going to the sender. The page asks people not to put voters' names, phones or addresses in it. One message a minute per person; at most 4,000 characters. Without email set up, or when sending fails, the page says so and gives the address to write to. Each message is audited as `feedback.send`, without its text.

**Exporting for the old spreadsheet.** On the admin page (organizers too), **Export for the old spreadsheet** downloads a CSV (`GET /api/admin/export/worked.csv`) of every open person a volunteer has worked on: a status other than Needs to be contacted, or at least one note. Cured people are left out, and an organizer gets only their precincts. The columns are `Name` (first, middle, last), `Status` as the volunteer set it, `Volunteer` (who has them: name, or email if none; blank if nobody), and `Notes` (one line per note: date, who wrote it, the text). No phone or address. A cell that starts with `=`, `+`, `-` or `@` gets a leading apostrophe so a spreadsheet does not run it as a formula. Each download is audited (`export.worked`).

**Inviting people.** On the admin page (site admins only), **Invite volunteers** takes pasted email addresses (one per line, or separated by commas or semicolons, at most 25 at a time) and emails each a message with step-by-step instructions: open the site, type this exact address, press the button to get a 6-digit code from Cloudflare, type it, then give a name. It is HTML in large type with an "Open the site" button, plus a plain-text copy, and replies go to the admin who invited. The app does not let anyone in: the address must also be in the Access policy first (docs/setup.md). The table under the form lists everyone invited: waiting for them to sign in, signed in (an account exists), or email not sent; **Send again** resends (not within 10 minutes of the last send), and **Copy addresses not signed in yet** gives the list to paste into the Access policy. Each batch that goes out also emails `admin@azld8cures.com` (`FEEDBACK_TO`) a reminder naming the addresses that were sent, with a link to the admin page, in case they are not on the Access policy yet; if that email fails, the invitations still stand. An address that already has an account is skipped. **Remove** takes an address off the list (it does not touch an account or the Access policy). Each send is audited (`invite.send`), each removal too (`invite.remove`).

**Map** (in the header, for everyone signed in) shows the open pool (not claimed, not cured) as pins on a map of LD8. A solid blue dot is a place the geocoder found exactly; a copper ring is a guess (the middle of a ZIP, a match the Census calls non-exact, or a place an admin set by hand); the legend above the map says so, and the shapes differ as well as the colors. Pins close together are a numbered group, blue if anyone in it is unclaimed and yellow (outlined, with dark numbers) if a volunteer has all of them; pressing one zooms in. Pressing a pin shows that person's name, status, days uncured, address and a **Claim** button under the map, and marks the pin. Claimed people who are not cured are pins as well, so a volunteer can see who is already working a block: a yellow dot with an outline in the text color, and a red one when nothing has happened for 3 days (a claim, a move, a status change or a note counts). Pressing one shows who has them, with their email (every signed-in volunteer sees it), and when they last did anything, instead of the Claim button. Claimed pins are not in the legend's "open pool" counts, and the filters narrow them too. People with no place at all are not on the map (Find people lists them); the page says how many pins are guesses. **Refresh** asks again. Above the map are the same filters as Find people (search, city, precinct, party, and the **Near me** box with its radius), applied to the pins. The filters live in the address (`?city=...&precinct=...`) and the header's links to Find people and Map carry them, so a filter chosen on one page is still there on the other. The edge of the district is a dashed line in the text color under the pins, from `public/map/ld8.json` (the Census's 2026 boundary for Arizona State Senate District 8, simplified to about 20 meters; `node tools/fetch-ld8.ts` fetches it again if the district is redrawn); the legend lists it. A volunteer who saved where they are sees that place on the map as a violet dot with a wide halo, listed in the legend as "You"; it is the same rounded place (about a mile) that only they ever see.

How it works. The page asks `GET /api/map?west&south&east&north` once for the district and a margin (not for each pan: each ask reads the whole pool's places from the `people_pool_place` index, about as many rows as there are people, so on the free plan the map loads once and refreshes by hand or after a claim). The answer is `[id, lat, lon, guess]` per person and nothing else: no names, addresses or phones. The Worker sends at most 20,000 and says when it stopped. The street map is a PMTiles file (a Protomaps basemap cut to a box around LD8) in the R2 bucket `cure-tracker-tiles`, served at `/tiles/ld8.pmtiles` by the Worker, with byte-range support, behind the same Access check as the rest. While the file is missing the page says so and draws only the pins. MapLibre, the PMTiles reader, the basemap styles (light and dark, made by `tools/map-style.ts` from `@protomaps/basemaps`) and the labels' fonts are served from `/lib/` on our own origin; the CSP allows nothing else. The map follows the theme (the basemap is the light or dark flavor).

The map is a canvas. Its pins cannot be reached with a keyboard or a screen reader, so the page says to use Find people, which lists the same people and can sort by distance.

## Audit log

Every change writes a row: who, when, what, which person. Joining, renaming, claims, releases, status changes, notes, assignments, reassignments, flag dismissals, uploads and what each upload cured, flagged or reopened. Admins read it newest first on the admin page.

## Limits and things not built

- The geocoder is the only outside service the Worker calls. The map's pins are the pool only (not a volunteer's own people), and show nothing about the volunteer's own place.
- The one email the app sends is "X assigned you a voter, check the site" (see **Assigning people** above); no other notifications and no SMS. Volunteers call or text from their own phones (the phone number is a `tel:` link).
- D1 and Worker limits matter for big uploads (docs/decisions.md, 17): the browser reads the file, each request carries about a hundred people and one SQL statement, and the comparison and the changes are set operations inside D1. `npm run bench` times it on a synthetic 10,000-person sheet. Staging costs about one row written per person and is cleared after.
